Quick-start checklist

A condensed version of what usually takes a resume-writing guide 50 pages to say.

    Writing your summary

    Open with your title and years of experience, then one line on the scale of what you manage (people, budget, revenue). Skip the objective statement — hiring managers want to know what you offer, not what you're looking for.

    Writing strong bullets

    Lead each bullet with an action verb, then the result, then the number if you have one: "Reduced onboarding time by 30% by redesigning the training program." Avoid "Responsible for..." — it describes the job, not your impact.

    Choosing what to cut

    One page for under 10 years of experience, two pages beyond that. Cut older roles down to a single line once they're more than 10–15 years back, and drop bullets that don't support the job you're applying for now.

    Cover letters

    Three short paragraphs: why this role, one concrete proof point from your background, and a low-pressure close. Name the company and role specifically — generic cover letters are easy to spot.

    References

    Always ask permission before listing someone. Include their relationship to you (manager, colleague, client) so the hiring manager knows what perspective they'll get.

    On ATS scanning

    Applicant tracking systems read left-to-right, top-to-bottom, and choke on tables, text boxes, and headers/footers. Every template in this tool avoids all three — but keep any custom sections you add in the same plain-text style.
